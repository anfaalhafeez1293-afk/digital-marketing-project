Week 1

Introduction

1
Session 1

Introduction to Digital Marketing

Learning objectives

  • Define digital marketing and distinguish major channels.
  • Explain why measurement and audience relevance matter.
What is digital marketing?

Digital marketing promotes products, services or brands through online channels such as search engines, websites, social media, email and digital advertising. It can help businesses communicate with audiences and measure actions such as visits, enquiries and purchases.

Why it matters today

Customers often use the internet to discover businesses, compare options and read reviews. Digital channels can help a business reach beyond its immediate area, explain its value and learn which messages are useful. Results depend on the offer, audience, execution and follow-up; online activity does not guarantee sales.

Channel overview

SEO supports visibility in unpaid search results; search ads buy placement for relevant queries; social media supports community and content; content marketing educates; email nurtures opted-in contacts; websites provide a reliable information and conversion point.

Career paths

Common roles include social media assistant, SEO specialist, content writer, paid ads assistant, email marketer, e-commerce assistant and marketing analyst. Each requires practice and a portfolio that accurately represents the learner's own work.

Pakistan and global applications

An illustrative Faisalabad clothing shop could post product information on Instagram, answer questions through WhatsApp Business and maintain a website. An international online store might use search content, email and paid campaigns together. These are examples, not verified case studies.

Practical activity

Choose a local business or clearly labelled fictional business. Write its target audience, two suitable digital channels and one metric to track.

ReflectionI learned that digital marketing uses online channels to reach relevant people. The right channel depends on the business goal and audience.
2
Session 2

Scope of Digital Marketing in Pakistan & Beyond

Learning objectives

  • Identify opportunities and trends without assuming every trend suits every business.
  • Evaluate a case study using goals, audience, channels and evidence.
Opportunities

Small businesses can use social platforms, search visibility, messaging apps and online stores to make their offers easier to discover. Freelancers may provide content, design, SEO, campaign support or reporting services. International work also requires clear communication, dependable delivery and awareness of client markets.

Trends to evaluate

Short-form video, creator partnerships, local search, e-commerce, first-party customer relationships and AI-assisted workflows are areas to explore. A trend should be judged by audience fit, cost, accessibility, privacy and measurable results.

Simple case-study method

Record the business problem, target audience, message, channel, evidence of activity, result if independently documented, and lessons. Separate verified facts from assumptions; do not invent sales or follower growth figures.

Pakistan-focused considerations

Language, mobile-first browsing, payment preferences, delivery coverage, trust and customer service can affect performance. Urdu, Roman Urdu and English may be appropriate for different audiences.

Practical activity

Select one Pakistani brand or local business. Find one public example of its digital presence and note the channel, intended audience and one thing you would test. Cite the source you used.

ReflectionI learned that opportunities exist in Pakistan and internationally, but a marketer should verify facts and choose channels based on customer needs.
Week 2

Marketing Funnels

3
Session 3

Marketing Funnels & Customer Journey

Learning objectives

  • Explain TOFU, MOFU and BOFU.
  • Use AIDA to organise messages across a customer journey.
What is a funnel?

A marketing funnel is a simplified model of how people move from first awareness of a brand toward consideration and a possible purchase. Not every customer follows the same straight path.

TOFU, MOFU and BOFU

Top of funnel (TOFU) introduces a problem or brand. Middle of funnel (MOFU) helps people compare options through guides, demonstrations or reviews. Bottom of funnel (BOFU) supports a decision with clear pricing, proof, FAQs or an easy enquiry process.

AIDA model

Attention earns notice; Interest gives a reason to continue; Desire connects benefits to needs; Action makes the next step clear. AIDA is a planning framework, not a guarantee of behaviour.

Customer journey map

Map stages, customer questions, touchpoints, possible barriers and a useful next action. Include post-purchase support and repeat-customer opportunities.

Practical activity

For a sample bakery, write one TOFU post, one MOFU piece of content and one BOFU call to action. Label it as a practice example.

ReflectionI learned that messages should match the customer's questions at each stage, rather than asking every viewer to buy immediately.
4
Session 4

Target Audience & Buyer Persona

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish market segments from individual personas.
  • Use demographic and psychographic information responsibly.
Segmentation

Market segmentation groups people by relevant shared traits such as location, needs, behaviours or purchasing context. Useful segments help a business make its offer more relevant.

Demographic and psychographic factors

Demographics may include age range, occupation or household context. Psychographics concern interests, priorities, motivations and attitudes. Use only information that is relevant, lawfully obtained and respectful.

Buyer persona

A persona is a research-informed summary of a likely customer, including goals, needs, concerns, preferred channels and decision factors. It is a working hypothesis and should be updated with evidence.

Using ChatGPT

AI can help organise notes or suggest persona questions, but it can invent details. Verify the output against interviews, customer feedback or credible research; do not enter private customer data into tools without permission.

Practical activity

Create a persona for a fictional local bookstore. Include goals, barriers, preferred channel and one assumption that needs validation.

ReflectionI learned that a useful persona is based on evidence and helps a business understand needs without stereotyping people.
Week 3

Freelancing Platforms

5
Session 5

Freelancing Platforms Overview

Learning objectives

  • Compare major freelancing platforms at a high level.
  • Identify what clients need before hiring a freelancer.
Platforms

Fiverr commonly presents packaged services or gigs; Upwork often uses proposals for posted work; Freelancer supports project bids and contests; Guru and PeoplePerHour also connect clients with freelancers. Platform rules, fees and features can change, so review current official guidance.

What clients look for

Clients value a clear service, relevant samples, realistic scope, communication, reliability, deadlines and honest claims. A profile should explain the problem solved rather than only list software names.

Professional and safe practice

Keep communication and payments within platform rules, read contracts, avoid paying suspicious upfront fees, protect account credentials and do not promise outcomes outside your control.

Practical activity

Compare two platforms using current official help pages. Note one suitable service format and one rule or fee to verify before working.

ReflectionI learned that platform choice should fit my service and working style, and that credibility depends on clear scope and reliable delivery.

Session 6

Profile Optimization & Gig Creation

Learning objectives

  • Write a focused profile and service offer.
  • Plan a readable portfolio thumbnail.
Profile structure

Use a specific headline, short introduction, service list, relevant skills, evidence of work and a clear next step. Avoid keyword stuffing and claims such as guaranteed results.

Gig or service description

State the client's problem, deliverables, process, information needed from the client, timeline assumptions, revisions and exclusions. Make the scope easy to understand.

Thumbnail design

A thumbnail should be legible on a phone, use a small number of visual elements and match the service. Canva or Looka can help with layout and branding, but review licensing and originality.

Practical activity

Draft a service listing for one beginner skill. Include title, audience, three deliverables, what you need from a client and one honest sample-work disclaimer.

ReflectionI learned that a clear and truthful service offer is stronger than vague promises or exaggerated claims.
Week 4

Content Marketing

7
Session 7

Content Marketing Fundamentals

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish blogging, copywriting and email marketing.
  • Plan content for educational, promotional and entertaining purposes.
Blogging

Blog posts can answer customer questions, explain a process or compare options. Strong articles use a clear structure, useful examples and reliable sources.

Copywriting

Copywriting uses concise persuasive language to help an audience understand an offer and take an appropriate action. Good copy is specific and avoids misleading claims.

Email marketing

Email can deliver updates, resources and offers to people who have opted in. Respect consent, privacy, unsubscribe requests and applicable rules.

Content types

Educational content teaches; promotional content explains an offer; entertaining content earns attention or builds community. A balanced plan supports the audience rather than repeating sales messages.

Practical activity

Create a three-post mini calendar for a fictional home bakery: one educational, one promotional and one entertaining post.

ReflectionI learned that each content type has a purpose, and a content plan should provide value as well as promote an offer.
8
Session 8

Article Writing Using AI

Learning objectives

  • Use AI for brainstorming without treating output as verified fact.
  • Edit for accuracy, clarity, originality and audience fit.
AI-assisted workflow

Start with a clear brief: audience, purpose, topic, tone, length and source requirements. Ask for an outline before drafting, then review every factual claim.

Editing and proofreading

Check spelling, grammar, flow, repeated phrases, unsupported claims and whether the article actually answers the reader's question. Tools such as Grammarly can flag issues but cannot replace human judgement.

Originality and responsibility

Add genuine examples and analysis, cite sources, respect copyright and follow the teacher's rules for AI use. Do not invent testimonials, statistics or quotations.

Practical activity

Ask an AI tool for an outline on a simple topic, then verify two factual claims from trustworthy sources and rewrite one paragraph in your own words. Keep notes of sources.

ReflectionI learned that AI can support drafting, but accuracy, editing and responsibility remain my job.
Week 5

SEO

9
Session 9

SEO Basics & On-Page SEO

Learning objectives

  • Explain search intent and basic on-page elements.
  • Improve a sample page title, description, URL and image alt text.
SEO basics

Search engine optimization helps search engines understand a page and helps people find useful content. No tool can guarantee a specific ranking.

Meta title and description

A descriptive page title helps identify the page. A meta description can summarise its value in search results, although search engines may choose different snippets.

URLs and headings

Use readable URLs and headings that accurately describe the page. Organise content logically and avoid unnecessary repetition of keywords.

Alt text

Alternative text communicates an image's meaningful information to people using screen readers and can help search engines understand image context. Describe the image's purpose; do not stuff keywords.

SEO writing tools

Tools such as Surfer may provide optimisation suggestions. Treat scores as guidance, not as proof of quality; the content must still help the reader.

Practical activity

Write a page title, meta description, readable URL and useful alt text for a fictional page selling handmade notebooks.

ReflectionI learned that on-page SEO combines clear structure, relevant content and accessibility rather than repeating keywords.
10
Session 10

Off-Page SEO & Link Building

Learning objectives

  • Explain backlinks and why relevance matters.
  • Recognise sustainable link-building methods and risky shortcuts.
Backlinks

A backlink is a link from another website to a page. Links can help people discover useful resources and may be a signal considered by search engines, but quality and context matter.

Ethical approaches

Useful approaches can include original research, helpful resources, relevant guest articles and genuine participation in professional communities. A link should make sense for the audience.

Forums and guest posting

Contribute useful answers rather than spamming links. Guest articles should be relevant, original and accepted under the publisher's guidelines.

Authority metrics

Third-party domain authority scores are comparative tools, not official Google ranking factors. Evaluate relevance, editorial standards, real audience and link quality instead of chasing a number.

Practical activity

Find two relevant websites that might reasonably reference a useful resource from a fictional business. Explain relevance and how you would approach each ethically.

ReflectionI learned that sustainable link building focuses on relevance and genuine value, not buying large numbers of low-quality links.
Week 6

Keyword Research

11
Session 11

Keyword Research Tools

Learning objectives

  • Understand keyword intent, search volume and CPC.
  • Use research tools to compare topics, not blindly follow scores.
Keyword intent

Informational searches seek knowledge; navigational searches seek a particular site; commercial investigation compares options; transactional searches show readiness to act. Intent can overlap.

Search volume

Volume estimates how often a phrase is searched over a period and location. It is an estimate, not a promise of traffic.

CPC

Cost per click is the amount an advertiser pays for a click in a pay-per-click campaign. Forecasts vary by competition, audience, location and time.

Tools

Ubersuggest and Google Keyword Planner can help discover phrases and estimate demand. Check settings and date ranges, and record where the data came from.

Practical activity

Choose five phrases for a fictional Jaranwala bakery. Label each intent and explain which one is most useful for a menu page and which for a blog post.

ReflectionI learned that keywords need to match the searcher's purpose and the page's job.

Session 12

SEO Audit & AI Blog Generation

Learning objectives

  • Check a page for basic technical and content issues.
  • Use AI to support a researched blog draft.
Basic audit

Review mobile usability, page titles, headings, broken links, image descriptions, page clarity, loading issues and whether the page answers the search intent. A small manual checklist is a useful starting point.

AI blog workflow

Define the audience and question, research reliable sources, prepare an outline, draft, verify claims, edit, add examples and include a clear title and useful headings.

Audit evidence

Record the page reviewed, date, tool or method, issue found and proposed fix. Do not claim a live audit was performed unless you actually used the site or tool.

Practical activity

Audit a practice page or a page you have permission to review. Record three observations, label any untested items and suggest one improvement.

ReflectionI learned that an SEO audit should produce specific, evidence-based improvements rather than just a score.

Social Media

Session 13

Social Media Platforms Overview

Learning objectives

  • Match platforms to audience and content purpose.
  • Distinguish a personal profile from a business presence.
Platform fit

Facebook can support community and local discovery; Instagram is visual; TikTok centres on short-form video; LinkedIn is professional; X supports public conversation; YouTube supports video discovery. Features and audience patterns change.

Business page versus personal profile

A personal profile represents an individual. Business tools may provide brand identity, team access, analytics and advertising features. Use the correct account type and follow platform policies.

Content and community

Publish consistently, use accessible captions, reply respectfully and track useful metrics. Avoid buying followers or copying other creators' work.

Practical activity

Choose one platform for a fictional local service. Explain the audience, a suitable post format and one metric that would indicate meaningful engagement.

ReflectionI learned that a platform should be selected for audience fit and business purpose, not simply because it is popular.

Session 14

Graphic Design with AI Tools

Learning objectives

  • Create a clear social post using a design tool.
  • Review AI-generated assets for accuracy, rights and brand fit.
Design basics

Use a clear visual hierarchy, readable type, sufficient contrast, consistent spacing and one primary message. Design for the actual placement and phone screen.

Tools

Canva provides templates and layout tools. Adobe Firefly and Leonardo AI offer AI-assisted image creation features. Tool capabilities and usage rights vary; check the current terms.

Responsible use

Check generated text, hands, product details, logos and visual artefacts. Do not present a fictional image as a real customer or real product photo. Use licensed assets and disclose mock work when appropriate.

Practical activity

Create a sample social post for a fictional business. Include a clear headline, one benefit and a call to action. Label it as a concept if it is not for a real client.

ReflectionI learned that design tools speed up production, but careful review and original judgement make the result usable.
Week 8

Marketing Strategy

15
Session 15

Social Media Marketing Strategy

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish strategy from tactics.
  • Build a basic content schedule with goals and measurement.
Strategy and tactics

Strategy explains the audience, objective, positioning and approach. Tactics are the specific actions, such as publishing a Reel or scheduling a post.

SMART goals

A useful goal is specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound. Select metrics connected to the objective: reach for awareness, qualified enquiries for lead generation or conversion rate for sales.

Scheduling

Notion can organise a content plan and Buffer can help schedule posts on supported platforms. Check current plan limits and platform integrations.

Review cycle

Plan, publish, measure, learn and adjust. Compare performance over a suitable period and account for context rather than judging one post alone.

Practical activity

Make a one-week calendar with three posts, each with audience, purpose, format, publish date and a success metric.

ReflectionI learned that strategy connects activities to goals, while a schedule makes execution easier to manage.
16
Session 16

Meta Ads Basics

Learning objectives

  • Understand the building blocks of a Meta ad campaign.
  • Draft audience-aware ad copy without misleading claims.
Campaign structure

Meta advertising commonly organises work into campaign, ad set and ad levels. Objectives, placements and settings available depend on the current platform setup.

Audience targeting

Audience choices may use location, broad or custom audiences and other permitted signals. Use lawful, respectful targeting and follow restrictions for sensitive categories.

Ad copy formats

A simple structure is hook, relevant benefit, supporting detail and clear call to action. Match the message to the landing page and avoid unsupported guarantees.

Testing and measurement

Set a budget you can afford, define a primary metric and compare controlled variations. Do not change many variables at once or treat a small sample as conclusive.

Practical activity

Write two ad-copy variations for a fictional online stationery shop. State the objective, intended audience and one metric to monitor. Do not launch paid ads as part of this exercise.

ReflectionI learned that ads need a clear objective, appropriate targeting, honest copy and careful measurement.
Week 9

Campaign Building

17
Session 17

Email Marketing Campaign Building

Learning objectives

  • Explain list building and drip campaigns.
  • Plan a simple consent-based email sequence.
List building

Build a list through clear opt-in forms, useful resources or customer sign-ups. Do not scrape or purchase lists without a lawful and appropriate basis.

Drip campaigns

A drip campaign sends a planned sequence of messages over time, often based on a person's sign-up or action. Each message should have a clear purpose and sensible frequency.

Tools

Mailchimp and ConvertKit/Kit offer email marketing features; product names and plans can change. Review current terms, privacy options and sending limits.

Good practice

Identify the sender, use accurate subject lines, protect subscriber data and provide an easy unsubscribe option. Monitor delivery, clicks, unsubscribes and meaningful conversions.

Practical activity

Draft a three-email welcome sequence for people who voluntarily subscribe to a fictional bakery's newsletter. Give each email a purpose and subject line.

ReflectionI learned that permission and relevance are central to respectful email marketing.
18
Session 18

Lead Generation Techniques

Learning objectives

  • Explain lead magnets and lead qualification.
  • Plan a simple, ethical lead-generation path.
Lead generation

Lead generation invites interested people to share contact details or make an enquiry. A useful process offers value and explains what happens next.

Lead magnets

Examples include a checklist, short guide, template or webinar. The resource should solve a real audience problem and be accurately described.

Qualification and follow-up

Ask only for information needed to respond. Define what makes an enquiry relevant, follow up at a reasonable time and protect contact data.

Case analysis

When reviewing a case, identify the audience, offer, channel, form, follow-up and reported evidence. Distinguish published results from assumptions.

Practical activity

Design a one-page checklist lead magnet for a fictional small business. Write the landing-page headline and explain what information the form genuinely needs.

ReflectionI learned that lead generation should exchange useful value for appropriate information and respect the person's choice.

YouTube Channel

Session 19

YouTube Channel Setup & Video SEO

Learning objectives

  • Identify core channel branding elements.
  • Improve video discoverability with accurate metadata.
Channel branding

A channel benefits from a clear name, profile image, banner, description and consistent topic. Make sure visual assets display well on mobile and desktop.

Video SEO

Use an accurate title, useful description, relevant chapters where appropriate and a thumbnail that represents the video. Viewer satisfaction and video quality matter; tags alone do not guarantee discovery.

Tools

TubeBuddy and vidIQ offer research or workflow features. Check current plans and recommendations, and use platform analytics to assess actual performance.

Accessibility

Add accurate captions, speak clearly and provide context for viewers who watch without sound.

Practical activity

Plan a YouTube video for a fictional business: title, audience question, three key points, description opening and thumbnail concept.

ReflectionI learned that channel branding and accurate, helpful metadata make a video easier to understand and discover.
20
Session 20

Short-Form Content Creation

Learning objectives

  • Plan a short vertical video with a clear message.
  • Understand the role and limits of editing tools.
Short-form structure

A short video can open with a relevant hook, deliver one useful idea, demonstrate or explain it, and end with a clear next step. Avoid misleading hooks.

Tools

Pictory and Runway ML provide AI-assisted video workflows; features, access and usage terms change. Review output carefully and use assets you have permission to use.

Reels and Shorts

Use vertical framing, readable on-screen text, captions and pacing suited to the message. Platform-specific dimensions and limits should be checked before publishing.

Quality checks

Verify facts, audio, subtitles, visual continuity, music rights and whether any synthetic content could mislead viewers.

Practical activity

Write a 20–30 second storyboard with four scenes for a fictional local service. Note the spoken line, visual and caption for each scene.

ReflectionI learned that a strong short video communicates one useful idea clearly and does not rely only on effects.
Week 11

Gig Polishing

21
Session 21

Freelancing Gig Polishing & Mock Client Chat

Learning objectives

  • Practise professional client discovery and scope-setting.
  • Recognise common errors and scam warning signs.
Client communication

Ask about goals, target audience, deliverables, deadline, budget range, approval process and existing assets. Summarise what you understood before starting.

Common mistakes

Vague scope, missed deadlines, poor file organisation, copied work, unrealistic promises and unclear revisions can damage trust.

Scam awareness

Be cautious of requests for passwords, suspicious payment arrangements, fees to unlock a job, unexplained overpayments or pressure to move off-platform against platform rules. Verify the client and use secure processes.

Professional boundaries

Agree on scope in writing, protect personal information and ask for clarification when requirements are unclear.

Practical activity

Write a short mock chat in which you ask a client three useful questions and summarise the agreed deliverables. Use a fictional client.

ReflectionI learned that clear communication and safe working practices are part of professional freelancing.

Session 22

Portfolio Website Setup

Learning objectives

  • Plan a simple portfolio structure.
  • Present work honestly with context and evidence.
Portfolio sections

A simple portfolio can include an introduction, services, selected work, process, contact details and a short biography. Keep navigation clear and check mobile usability.

Wix and Notion

Both can support portfolio-style pages in different ways. Review current publishing options, privacy settings, custom-domain options and plan limits.

Show work responsibly

For each sample, explain the brief, your role, tools used and what you learned. Label practice work and fictional brands clearly; never claim someone else's work or invented client results as your own.

Quality checks

Test links, spelling, loading, contrast, image permissions and contact methods before sharing.

Practical activity

Sketch a three-section portfolio for your current skill level. Choose two honest sample projects you could create and explain how you would label them.

ReflectionI learned that a portfolio should make my skills easy to evaluate and clearly distinguish practice work from client work.
Week 12

Earning Plan

23
Session 23

Proposal Writing & Earning Plan

Learning objectives

  • Structure a concise proposal around a client's needs.
  • Set realistic practice goals and track progress.
Proposal structure

A proposal can include the client's goal, your understanding of the task, proposed approach, deliverables, timeline assumptions, price or pricing method, client responsibilities and next step.

Freelancing strategy

Choose a service that matches current skills, practise it, create relevant samples, request feedback and apply selectively. Do not promise outcomes you cannot control.

Income goals

Set learning and activity goals before assuming income. Track proposals sent, responses, completed work, time spent and expenses. Income is uncertain and may vary.

Ethics and reliability

Be clear about scope, deadlines, revisions and what is excluded. Use contracts or platform agreements and follow relevant rules.

Practical activity

Draft a one-page proposal for a fictional client. Include the objective, deliverables, timeline, assumptions and one question that needs client confirmation.

ReflectionI learned that a professional proposal focuses on the client's goal and explains the work clearly. Earnings should be planned realistically.
24
Session 24

Final Presentation & Mock Interview

Learning objectives

  • Present a project clearly and answer questions with evidence.
  • Identify professional habits that support confidence.
Presentation structure

Introduce the problem, audience, objective, approach, sample work, learning and next steps. Keep slides or speaking notes readable and focused.

Dress and presentation style

Choose clean, appropriate clothing for the setting, speak at a comfortable pace, maintain respectful body language and use simple language. Professionalism is about preparation and communication, not expensive clothing.

Confidence building

Practise aloud, time the presentation, prepare for likely questions and acknowledge what you still need to learn. If you do not know an answer, explain how you would find it.

Portfolio walkthrough

Show only work you created or are authorised to share. Explain decisions and limitations honestly, including whether a result is a mock-up or a real measured outcome.

Practical activity

Prepare a two-minute presentation introducing one sample project. Answer: What was the goal? Who was it for? What did you create? How would you measure success?

ReflectionI learned that preparation, clarity and honesty are more valuable than pretending to know everything.
